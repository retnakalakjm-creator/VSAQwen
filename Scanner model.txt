Because VSA requires calculating a Volume Moving Average (usually 20–50 periods) and Spread averages, you cannot just download today's candle; you must also fetch a tiny lookback buffer to ensure your indicators remain accurate.

The Lookback & Append Strategy
Instead of downloading the entire history, your new script workflow should look like this:
Check if CSV exists: If it doesn't, do a one-time historical download.
Read the last row: Find the most recent date already saved in the CSV.
Download with a buffer: Set the start date of yfinance to 50 days before that last saved date up to today.
Drop duplicates and append: Calculate your VSA features on this new chunk, drop the overlapping lookback rows, and append only the brand-new rows to the CSV.

Managing structural Swing Highs and Swing Lows (like market structure shifts or break of structure) across a watchlist introduces a specific challenge: a new swing high might only be confirmed 3 to 5 bars after it actually happens.
If you cut your scan off exactly at your last saved date, you risk missing a swing point that was forming right on the edge of your last file update.
To solve this smoothly, you must use a Truncate-and-Recalculate strategy combined with a Watchlist Loop.
The Truncate-and-Recalculate Strategy
Instead of appending strictly to the end of the file, you delete a small safety window (e.g., the last 20 rows) from your existing CSV, and recalculate everything from that point forward.
This ensures that any lagging swing points or structural transitions are perfectly captured and updated without ever needing to re-scan your entire multi-year history.

To scale your scanner to 50–100 stocks without facing major delays or getting blocked by yfinance, you need to run the scanning process in parallel and avoid calling yf.download() in a single-threaded loop.
If you scan 100 stocks sequentially, network latency alone will make the process take minutes. By utilizing Python’s multiprocessing library, you can distribute the watchlist across all your computer's CPU cores, cutting the execution time down to just a few seconds.
🚀 The Scaled Architecture
Process Pooling: Instead of waiting for Ticker A to download and save before starting Ticker B, your computer will process 4 to 16 tickers at the exact same fraction of a second.
Rate-Limit Prevention: yfinance can throttle your IP address if you flood it with requests. Splitting the downloads into an incremental strategy naturally reduces the data payload size per request, making your traffic look cleaner.

Because you are scanning Weekly Charts, data sizes are tiny, but processing the data safely requires keeping a strict calendar anchor.
A standard yfinance download for weekly data labels each candle by the start day of that week (Monday). If you run your scanner on a Wednesday or Friday, yfinance returns an incomplete, live-forming candle for the current week.
To prevent this live, moving candle from polluting your historical structure files, your scanner should drop the live, unclosed week during saving but still check it to see if any VSA alerts are currently firing on the dashboard.
